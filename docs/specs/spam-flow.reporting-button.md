Spam emails have several telltale signs you can learn to spot quickly. Here's a practical breakdown.

## How to identify spam

**Sender red flags**
- The "from" address doesn't match the supposed sender. A bank email coming from `secure-alerts@random-domain.info` rather than the bank's real domain is a giveaway.
- Slightly misspelled domains meant to fool you at a glance: `paypa1.com`, `amaz0n-support.net`, `micros0ft.com`.
- Display name says one thing ("Apple Support") but the actual email address is gibberish or unrelated.

**Content red flags**
- Urgency and threats: "Your account will be closed in 24 hours," "Immediate action required," "Suspicious login detected."
- Generic greetings: "Dear Customer," "Dear User" instead of your name.
- Too-good-to-be-true offers: lottery winnings, inheritances, free prizes, miracle cures.
- Requests for sensitive info: passwords, card numbers, verification codes. Legitimate companies almost never ask for these by email.
- Poor grammar and spelling, awkward phrasing, or odd formatting.
- Mismatched or hidden links: hover over a link (don't click) and check whether the real URL matches the visible text.

**Technical red flags**
- Unexpected attachments, especially `.zip`, `.exe`, `.scr`, or files pushing you to "enable macros."
- You never signed up for or contacted this sender.
- The email is sent to many recipients, or the "to" field is blank/undisclosed.

## Distinct properties that separate spam from legitimate mail

The core difference is that spam is unsolicited and usually sent in bulk with some ulterior motive: selling something dubious, phishing for credentials, spreading malware, or running a scam. Legitimate mail generally addresses you specifically, comes from a verifiable domain, doesn't manufacture panic, and doesn't ask you to hand over secrets or money on short notice.

A useful mental filter: *did I expect this, do I know the real sender, and does it want me to click, pay, or reveal something urgently?* Two or more "no/yes" answers there means treat it as suspicious.

## How to get rid of them

**Immediate actions**
- Use your email provider's **"Report spam"** or **"Report phishing"** button rather than just deleting. This trains the filter and helps block future messages from that source.
- **Don't unsubscribe** from obvious spam or scam emails. For spam from unknown/illegitimate senders, clicking unsubscribe often just confirms your address is active and invites more. (Unsubscribing is fine for legitimate newsletters you actually signed up for.)
- Never click links, download attachments, or reply.

**Reduce future spam**
- Block the sender if the same address keeps hitting you.
- Set up filters/rules to auto-route messages with certain traits to trash or spam.
- Use a secondary or "throwaway" email address for sign-ups, promotions, and one-off registrations, keeping your primary address more private.
- Consider email aliases (many providers and services like Apple's "Hide My Email" offer these) so you can disable an alias if it starts getting spammed.
- Avoid posting your email address publicly in plain text where bots can scrape it.
- Keep your provider's spam filtering on; major providers (Gmail, Outlook, etc.) filter most spam automatically.

**If you already interacted with one**
- If you clicked a link and entered credentials, change that password immediately (and anywhere else you reused it), and enable two-factor authentication.
- If you opened an attachment, run a malware scan.
- Watch for unusual account activity.
